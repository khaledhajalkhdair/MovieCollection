Criterion Collection

No. 001

Come and See

1985 · Elem Klinov

●●●●●

Pretty horrific film about the horrors and futility of war. Horribly disturbing, and left me with a pit in my stomach. Definitely would not recommend this one unless your life is going swimmingly.

There’s a scene where a cow gets shot, and I later found out that they just actually shot a cow, which somewhat dampened things for me. I guess things were just done differently back then. Fun watch for the whole family!

No. 002

House

1977 · Nobuhiko Obayashi

●●●●○

A fun, wild Japanese horror-comedy romp. If you were given 50 years, you would still never guess what could happen next at any moment in this movie. It feels like a fever dream in all of the best ways, and it has a banging soundtrack.

I need the shirt of that cat poster.

No. 003

The Royal Tenenbaums

2001 · Wes Anderson

●●●●○

Very good Wes Anderson movie! This is arguably a film from before he became the caricature of a director that he is today with his choice of center-framing and pastel colors (not that I dislike that, I am quite a big fan of his). In contrast to that style, this film feels very grounded and focused on character drama, very similar in vein to his first film Bottle Rocket.

No. 004

Late Spring

1949 · Yasujiro Ozu

●●●●●

My favorite Ozu film (of the two that I have admittedly seen). A simple, yet effective story complete with beautiful cinematography and a quiet, subdued tone that makes the film feel comfortable, and its events inevitable.

Beautiful scenery and an even more beautiful story.

No. 005

After Hours

1985 · Martin Scorsese

●●●●○

A young man’s quest for companionship in late-night 80s New York ends in chaos. This Martin Scorsese-directed joint is a great watch, particularly with a group of friends. Its tone is fascinating; so alluring and mysterious, especially at first, before quickly layering in more and more levels of comedy.

This is genuinely how dating in your 20s feels.

No. 006

The Grand Budapest Hotel

2014 · Wes Anderson

●●●●●

A beautiful and hilarious film. Might be one of my favorite Wes Anderson films (next to Fantastic Mr. Fox). The tone of this film is so light, airy, and joyous; and then it isn’t. I appreciate this tonal contrast a lot, and think it works to leave a lasting impression on viewers.

Love the relationship between Gustave and his Lobby Boy.

No. 007

Dazed and Confused

1993 · Richard Linklater

●●●●○

A film that elicits the feeling of late-night high school get-togethers all too well, and manages to elicit feelings of nostalgia despite its setting. Timeless stuff.

I didn't know friendship existed in the 70s.
